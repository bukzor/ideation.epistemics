# Distrust enters through ill-grounded agent claims; the repair is to record grounding accurately

The owner ranked four routes. Ill-grounded agent claims lead, and the
root cause to attack is the record of grounding, not the claims:

> [!@bukzor] f65fbdf3#L815
> ill-grounded agent claims is top of the pile. They're never *un*grounded but tend to be either too strongly grounded in inference from an answer or comment of mine, or simply poor inference that's later taken to be ground truth. I think the root cause to attack there is ensure grounding is accurately recorded, such that we can tell the difference between all those post-hoc.

Wrong derivations feed the first route and are tolerable while
weightless; what is missing is the pathway to review them once they
bear weight:

> [!@bukzor] f65fbdf3#L815
> they would be entirely tolerable as long as its obvious to all involved to what extent they were agent-inferred and weight-bearing. Initially, such claims bear no weight so it's not very worthwhile to prevent/review them at that time. But later, when it _is_ worthwhile, we don't have a good pathway.

The owner's own shorthand costs turns, not correctness:

> [!@bukzor] f65fbdf3#L815
> it does tend to take six turns to get them properly amended due to (understandable) agent reluctance to revise proactively. More of a turn-waste pain than a correctness pain.

Drifted imports have not yet turned up; the owner expects to need a scan
for dependents that no longer match, prioritized for revision.

What each route demands of the model: a ground record fine enough to
tell inference-from-remark from stipulation and from poor inference
(`GROUND_RECORD`); a queue that surfaces a claim when it becomes
load-bearing and defers it until then (`DEFER_NOT_DISMISS`); the
steelman move granted unasked (`AGENT_REDUCTIONS`); and the conflict component
(`CONFLICT`).

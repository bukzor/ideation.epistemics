# Asked to find fabrications, agents flag the owner's contributions and pass the real ones

> [!@bukzor] f65fbdf3#L815
> I find that paradoxically, when agents are asked scan claims for what looks fabricated, they tend to pick out my content, and scan right past the actual fabrications. My theory is that this happens because the "actual fabrications" carry no cognitive dissonance, while _my_ contributions look "out of left field" by comparison.

Consequence for the model: plausibility is not evidence of grounding,
and a scan for the implausible selects against the owner. Any pruning or
fabrication check must read the record of grounding, never the text's
fit with its neighbours.

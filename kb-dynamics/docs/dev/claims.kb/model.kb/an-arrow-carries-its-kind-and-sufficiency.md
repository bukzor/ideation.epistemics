# An arrow carries its kind and its sufficiency, so post hoc reading can tell grounds apart

Each `why:` arrow records what the citing claim takes from the cited
one -- stipulation, entailment, inference, or association -- and whether
the cited grounds together entail the claim or only motivate it.
Provenance stays where it is: the body's verbatim callouts and the
session id. Whether this record is enough to tell an ill-grounded claim
apart after the fact is the exploration's to show; a real ledger where
it fails voids this claim.

Two rulings converge here. `DRIFT_ENTRY` says the repair for
ill-grounded claims is a record accurate enough to distinguish, after
the fact, an inference from an owner remark from a stipulation and from
a poor inference. `DERIVED_TRUST` says unread trust needs "entirely
rests" and "rigorously correct", neither of which a bare `why:` arrow
records.

Declined: provenance on the arrow, which the callout convention already
carries; and nothing new on the arrow, which leaves the kind to be read
from prose -- the post-hoc work the owner wants to stop doing.
